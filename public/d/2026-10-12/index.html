Meditation

First Reading. Paul reads Abraham’s two sons as two covenants. The son of the slave woman, born according to the flesh, stands for Sinai and bondage; the son of the free woman, born by promise, for the Jerusalem above, our mother. We are her children, so we must stand fast in the freedom Christ gave.

Psalm. The psalm praises the Lord’s name from sunrise to sunset. He is high above the nations, yet he stoops to look on heaven and earth, and he lifts the poor from the dust and the needy from the dunghill.

Gospel. Jesus calls his generation evil for demanding a sign and promises only the sign of Jonah. The queen of the south came far to hear Solomon, and Nineveh repented at Jonah’s preaching; a greater than Solomon and a greater than Jonah is here.

Sarah’s promised child, Jonah and Solomon all pointed ahead to what Christ now accomplishes, and the God of the psalm lifts the lowly. Stop asking for more proof: repent of one specific habit today and live as someone free.

Asked for a sign, Jesus points in Luke 11:29–32 to Jonah; Newman shows the old signs answered by one from the earth.

On a second occasion they asked again, Sadducees as well as Pharisees: they "came, and tempting, desired Him that He would show them a sign from heaven." Joshua had stopped the sun and moon "in the sight of Israel;" Samuel had brought thunder at harvest time; they asked for a similar miracle. They asked for a sign from heaven; He answered still by promising a Sign from the earth,—a Sign like his, who was "three days and three nights in the whale's belly."

— John Henry Newman, Parochial and Plain Sermons, vol. 6, sermon 9, “The Gospel Sign Addressed to Faith,” pp. 105–106
