Meditation

Philippians 2:10–11 gives Jesus the title “Lord”; the Catechism traces it from the Greek Old Testament’s rendering of God’s name.

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament, the ineffable Hebrew name YHWH, by which God revealed himself to Moses, is rendered as Kyrios, “Lord.” From then on, “Lord” becomes the more usual name by which to indicate the divinity of Israel’s God. The New Testament uses this full sense of the title “Lord” both for the Father and-what is new-for Jesus, who is thereby recognized as God Himself. . . . By attributing to Jesus the divine title “Lord,” the first confessions of the Church’s faith affirm from the beginning that the power, honor, and glory due to God the Father are due also to Jesus, because “he was in the form of God,” and the Father manifested the sovereignty of Jesus by raising him from the dead and exalting him into his glory.

— Catechism of the Catholic Church, §§446, 449 (the title “Lord”)
